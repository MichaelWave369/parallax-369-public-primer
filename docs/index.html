Human Authority Retained

A specification is not an implementation.

An implementation is not evidence.

Evidence does not authorize claims beyond its scope.

One governed sequence

Three stages. Three different jobs.

Each stage produces a distinct artifact and passes through a human-controlled gate. No stage is allowed to impersonate another.

What are we actually proposing, and what must be true?

Turn the idea into a controlling specification.

Preserve the original intent, separate facts from assumptions, identify human authority, make uncertainty visible, and write acceptance criteria that can actually be evaluated.

  • Facts, assumptions, hypotheses, proposals, unknowns
  • Scope, requirements, risks, and non-scope
  • Testable acceptance criteria
  • Decision history and Stage 6 handoff
Open the Stage 3 guide →

What is the smallest honest candidate we can build?

Implement without rewriting the target.

Map every requirement to a component and a status. Preserve deviations, dependencies, build instructions, mocks, simulations, failures, and deferred work.

  • Requirement-to-implementation traceability
  • Implemented, mocked, simulated, deferred, failed
  • Reproducible commands and artifact identity
  • Limitations and Stage 9 evidence handoff
Open the Stage 6 guide →

What demonstrably works, where, and under which conditions?

Preserve the evidence—including failure.

Run the acceptance tests, retain observations, document the environment, identify unsupported claims, and give the authorized human a scoped release recommendation.

  • Pass, fail, blocked, inconclusive, not run
  • Raw observations and reproduction limits
  • Supported and unsupported claim matrix
  • Human release decision and rationale
