# Synthetic Example: Community Seed-Swap Station

This fictional non-software example demonstrates the public Parallax 3–6–9 Method with a small physical setup. It is not a real field report, agricultural recommendation, or safety certification.

## Original idea

> Create a portable table-top station that helps neighbors sort and exchange labeled seed packets during a two-hour community event.

---

# 3 — Specify

## Purpose

Provide a simple, reusable physical station that makes six seed categories easy to identify and keeps incoming and outgoing packets organized during a small supervised event.

## Facts

- The candidate is a physical, non-powered station.
- The synthetic event lasts two hours.
- Two fictional volunteers are available for setup and supervision.
- The candidate does not evaluate seed quality, legality, safety, viability, or suitability.

## Assumptions

- The event occurs indoors on a stable table.
- Visitors bring sealed or folded packets with written labels.
- Volunteers can redirect unlabeled or leaking packets away from the station.
- Six broad categories are sufficient for the demonstration.

## Unknowns

- Real participant volume.
- Whether category names are equally understandable to all visitors.
- How the materials perform outdoors or in wet conditions.
- Whether local rules apply to particular seeds or exchanges.

## Non-scope

- seed testing or germination guarantees;
- legal or regulatory approval;
- food, allergen, invasive-species, or biosecurity screening;
- unsupervised operation;
- weatherproof or permanent installation;
- inventory tracking or participant identity collection.

## Requirements

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `REQ-01` | Two volunteers can assemble the station in no more than 15 minutes using the provided parts. |
| `REQ-02` | The station provides six visibly distinct labeled categories. |
| `REQ-03` | Each category holds at least 20 standard paper seed packets without collapsing. |
| `REQ-04` | A first-time visitor can identify the correct category for a sample packet without verbal assistance. |
| `REQ-05` | The station includes a clearly labeled quarantine tray for damaged, leaking, or unlabeled packets. |
| `REQ-06` | The full candidate packs into one reusable container with a total mass below 9 kg. |

## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Method | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| `AC-01` | Time two volunteers from closed container to ready state. | Ready in 15 minutes or less. |
| `AC-02` | Inspect the assembled station. | Six category labels are present and not dependent on color alone. |
| `AC-03` | Load 20 synthetic packets into each category. | No category collapses or spills during a five-minute observation. |
| `AC-04` | Ask five first-time fictional participants to place three sample packets. | At least 13 of 15 placements are correct without verbal help. |
| `AC-05` | Inspect the station from the visitor side. | Quarantine tray and purpose statement are visible. |
| `AC-06` | Pack and weigh the candidate. | One container; total mass below 9 kg. |

## Stage 3 gate

**Candidate decision:** Proceed to a small supervised prototype. Do not claim public-event readiness, regulatory compliance, or seed safety.

---

# 6 — Build

## Candidate components

- one folding tabletop display frame;
- six removable packet bins;
- six text-and-symbol category cards;
- one red-bordered quarantine tray labeled in words;
- one setup card with a numbered diagram;
- one reusable carrying container;
- synthetic paper packets for testing.

## Traceability

| Requirement | Candidate component | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `REQ-01` | Numbered setup card and tool-free frame | Implemented — unproven |
| `REQ-02` | Six text-and-symbol category cards | Implemented — unproven |
| `REQ-03` | Six packet bins | Implemented — unproven |
| `REQ-04` | Category wording and symbols | Implemented — unproven |
| `REQ-05` | Quarantine tray with written purpose | Implemented — unproven |
| `REQ-06` | Single carrying container | Implemented — unproven |

## Deviations

- The original concept proposed laminated cards. The candidate uses heavy paper inside reusable sleeves to reduce cost.
- The candidate has not been exposed to rain, wind, heat, or an actual crowd.

## Build receipt

- All materials are generic and fictionalized for the example.
- No real seed packets or participant information were used.
- The candidate is suitable only for the declared synthetic test.

## Stage 6 handoff

The proving ground receives the assembled candidate, packed container, setup card, synthetic packets, scale, timer, observation sheet, and acceptance criteria.

---

# 9 — Prove

## Synthetic test conditions

- indoor room;
- stable 180 cm table;
- two fictional volunteers;
- five fictional first-time participants;
- 120 synthetic packets;
- no public event and no real seeds.

## Results

| Criterion | Result | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| `AC-01` | Pass | Setup completed in 11 minutes 42 seconds. |
| `AC-02` | Pass | Six written labels and distinct symbols were present. |
| `AC-03` | Pass | All bins held 20 synthetic packets for five minutes without collapse. |
| `AC-04` | Fail | 11 of 15 placements were correct. Two category names were repeatedly confused. |
| `AC-05` | Pass | Quarantine tray was visible and correctly described by all five participants. |
| `AC-06` | Pass | Candidate packed into one container with a synthetic measured mass of 7.4 kg. |

## Supported claims

Within the fictional test conditions, the candidate:

- can be assembled by two people within 15 minutes;
- provides six text-and-symbol categories;
- holds the declared synthetic packet load;
- includes a visible quarantine area;
- packs into one container below the stated mass limit.

## Unsupported claims

The evidence does not establish that:

- category labels are sufficiently understandable;
- the station works at a real public event;
- the station is weatherproof, safe, legal, accessible to every visitor, or suitable for unsupervised use;
- any seed is viable, permitted, correctly identified, or appropriate to exchange.

## Release recommendation

**Revise before public-event use.** Return `REQ-04` to Stage 3 for wording review and Stage 6 for a revised labeling candidate. Preserve the failed result rather than lowering the acceptance criterion after testing.

## Lesson

A physical object can look complete and still fail its most human-centered requirement. The pipeline treats participant understanding as evidence to test, not an aesthetic assumption.
