# Contributing

Thank you for helping make the Parallax 3–6–9 Public Primer clearer, safer, and more useful.

## Before contributing

Read:

1. `PUBLIC_RELEASE_BOUNDARY.md`
2. `GOVERNANCE.md`
3. `SECURITY.md`

Do not contribute material copied, adapted, summarized, or reconstructed from private Parallax repositories, conversations, prompts, projects, or connected accounts.

## Good contributions

Useful public contributions include:

- clearer explanations of the three stages;
- accessible diagrams and educational examples;
- blank public templates;
- synthetic end-to-end demonstrations;
- tests for public schemas or static-site behavior;
- corrections to ambiguity, grammar, accessibility, or navigation;
- translations that preserve governance and claim boundaries.

## Pull-request requirements

A pull request should include:

- a concise explanation of the change;
- the public problem it solves;
- affected files and compatibility notes;
- tests or a synthetic example when relevant;
- confirmation that no private Parallax material or personal data was used;
- confirmation that the contributor has the right to license the work.

Suggested declaration:

> I confirm that this contribution was created for the public primer, contains no private Parallax material or personal data, and may be distributed under the repository's applicable licenses.

## Writing guidance

Prefer language that is:

- specific rather than mystical;
- transparent about uncertainty;
- clear about human authority;
- explicit about evidence scope;
- welcoming to users of different tools and platforms;
- careful not to imply certification or guaranteed outcomes.

## Examples

All examples must be fictional or synthetic. Do not merely rename a real private project. Invent the scenario, data, requirements, and evidence specifically for publication.

## Status labels

Use the vocabulary in `GOVERNANCE.md`: Proposed, Candidate, Implemented, Simulated, Mocked, Deferred, Failed, Verified in scope, and Unknown.

## Review

Maintainers may request changes for clarity, safety, licensing, governance, accessibility, or public-release-boundary reasons. A technically correct contribution may still be declined when its provenance or publication safety cannot be established.
